------
My name is Jacob D. J. Peters. I am a PhD candidate at the Forest School at the Yale School of the Environment working with professor of silviculture and applied forest ecology, Dr. Mark Ashton, and professor of meteorology and remote sensing, Dr. Xuhui Lee. My dissertation research is centered around lianas (woody vines), their distributions, the potential to detect them using drone remote sensing, and their impact on tree growth in temperate forests. In the broadest sense, my work aims to test innovative methods in forest monitoring to inform our knowledge of regeneration following disturbance and management, inform climate smart forestry and forest management in general. While working toward this goal, I enjoy studying forest phenomena at varying scales: from continental-scale species distribution modeling to UAV (drone) remote sensing and, most importantly, field work and experiments to corroborate findings. I am also involved in guiding several other projects for our current and recently-graduated masters of forestry and PhD students. These projects range from mapping and understanding biomass changes in forest fragments surrounding cocoa plantations in Ghana, understory regeneration in teak plantations in Ghana, Sal forest cover change in Bangladesh, the effect of fuel reduction treatments on wildfire severity in the American West, and street tree diversity in urban canopies of New England. 

My teaching focuses on forest ecology and management, forest mensuration, botany, and silviculture while leaning heavily on code-based environmental data science, remote sensing, and geospatial analyses. At Yale, I have served as the teaching fellow for Silviculture, Forest Dynamics, the Research and Communication Seminar for Masters of Forestry students, and Observing Earth From Space (remote sensing).
